# Everyday professional emails

Default to short, confident prose. A friendly professional tone does not require long openings, forced gratitude, or management jargon.

## Pick the purpose

| Purpose | Opening and structure | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| **Request / approval** | State what you need, then why; share options only if relevant | Explicit ask and date only when known |
| **Meeting invitation** | Say why the meeting is needed and what it should accomplish; add a few topics if helpful | State needed preparation, or invite questions |
| **Follow-up / reminder** | Identify the earlier conversation or request, then what remains open | Ask for a status or response without guilt or pressure |
| **Diplomatic disagreement** | Identify the shared objective, then the specific concern and evidence | Propose a constructive next step |
| **Recognition / thanks** | Name the action and its impact, without excessive praise | Simple personal thank-you |
| **Change request** | State what is changing, who is affected, and what needs to happen | Specific next action or offer to clarify |

## Useful patterns

**Request:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Could you help us with [specific request]? We're trying to [brief reason].
>
> Let me know if you need anything else from my side.
>
> Thanks,  
> [Your Name]

**Follow-up:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Following up on [specific item]. Have you had a chance to review it?
>
> Let me know if you need any additional details.
>
> Thanks,  
> [Your Name]

These are **patterns**, not universal wording. If the context supports a more specific sentence, use it instead.

## Subject guidance

Use a descriptive subject that communicates the topic or action. Preserve existing `Re:` subjects on replies. Avoid urgency labels unless the user indicates urgency.

## Avoid

- "Gentle reminder" when "Following up" is clearer.
- "Kindly do the needful," "circle back," "touch base," and lengthy courtesies.
- Assuming the recipient already approved something or agreed to a deadline.
- Adding a table just to look organized when two sentences would do.
